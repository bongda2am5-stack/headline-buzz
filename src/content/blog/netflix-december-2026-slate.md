December is the month Netflix stops holding back. The streamer is closing out 2026 with a slate for every kind of viewer: a beloved Jane Austen classic reimagined as a limited series, an Oscar-contending Spanish drama, a documentary from one of America's most acclaimed filmmakers, a long-awaited animated film from the director of The Incredibles, a David Fincher crime comedy, the final season of a global hit, and live NFL football on Christmas Day. Here is your guide, date by date.

## December 1: All four Shrek films are back

The month opens with a gift for anyone who grew up quoting an ogre. All four Shrek films arrive on December 1: Shrek, Shrek 2, Shrek the Third, and Shrek Forever After. It is the kind of catalog addition that quietly becomes the most-watched thing on the service, because Shrek works for a family movie night and for adults revisiting it alone. December 1 also brings A Knight's Tale, Big Fish, and Edward Scissorhands, but Shrek is the headline.

## December 2: La Bola Negra, the awards-season heavyweight

December 2 brings La Bola Negra, the Spanish drama building awards momentum all year. The film follows three generations of men across nearly a century of Spanish history, weaving desire, pain, and love into an epic that premiered at the Cannes Film Festival in May, where directors Javier Calvo and Javier Ambrossi won the Best Director award. It has since collected praise on the festival circuit and is now talked about as a major contender in the 2027 Oscar race, not just for Best International Feature but beyond it.

## December 3: Pride and Prejudice, the new Austen

One day later comes the month's biggest television event. Netflix's new Pride and Prejudice arrives December 3 as a limited series, with Emma Corrin as Elizabeth Bennet and Jack Lowden as Mr. Darcy. Written by Dolly Alderton and directed by Euros Lyn, it follows Elizabeth's clash with the wealthy Fitzwilliam Darcy as pride and ego slowly give way to something deeper, faithful to Jane Austen's 1813 novel. Austen adaptations live or die on their casting, and Corrin and Lowden are an intriguing pairing: Corrin brings a sharp, modern energy to Elizabeth, while Lowden has the stillness and gravity Darcy needs. Olivia Colman and Rufus Sewell co-star. Watch it early: if you have ever argued about which Pride and Prejudice is the best one, this series will restart that debate.

## December 4: Ava DuVernay's 14th

December 4 brings 14th, Ava DuVernay's new documentary about the Fourteenth Amendment to the United States Constitution. It arrives a decade after her acclaimed documentary 13th, which examined the Thirteenth Amendment and mass incarceration, and it works as a companion piece: where 13th looked at the amendment that abolished slavery, 14th examines the amendment that promised citizenship and due process, and asks what that promise has meant in practice. The film features interviews with voices ranging from Representative Alexandria Ocasio-Cortez and Senator Alex Padilla to Stacey Abrams and conservative author Donald T. Critchlow.

## December 18: Ray Gunn, 30 years in the making

Mid-December belongs to animation. Ray Gunn, Brad Bird's new animated film, arrives December 18, and it has been a long time coming: Bird, the director of The Incredibles, Ratatouille, and The Iron Giant, has spent 30 years trying to make this movie. Ray Gunn is a sci-fi noir set in a retro-futuristic world where humans and aliens coexist, following private detective Ray Gunn, voiced by Sam Rockwell, as he works a case involving murder and a multimedia star named Venus Nova. Scarlett Johansson and Tom Waits round out the voice cast. For animation fans, this is the month's must-see.

## December 23: Cliff Booth is back

Two days before Christmas, David Fincher delivers one of the most anticipated films of the year. The Further Mis-Adventures of Cliff Booth arrives December 23, bringing Brad Pitt back as the stuntman he played in Quentin Tarantino's Once Upon a Time in Hollywood. Tarantino wrote the screenplay, Fincher directs, and Timothy Olyphant also returns. The story picks up eight years after the 2019 film, following Cliff as he works odd jobs around 1970s Los Angeles. This is the film to watch on the quiet night before Christmas Eve, when the presents are wrapped and the house is finally still.

## December 24: Emily in Paris says goodbye

Christmas Eve brings the end of an era. Emily in Paris returns December 24 for its sixth and final season, with all ten episodes dropping at once. Lily Collins is back as Emily Cooper, and this time the season takes her to Greece and Monaco before bringing her back to Paris. Six seasons in, Emily in Paris has become one of Netflix's most reliable comfort watches: beautiful locations, complicated romance, and fashion that does half the storytelling. It is also the perfect Christmas Eve binge: light, glamorous, and easy to watch with family.

## December 25: Christmas Day football, live on Netflix

And then there is Christmas Day itself. Netflix will stream two live NFL games on December 25: the Buffalo Bills against the Denver Broncos, and the Green Bay Packers against the Chicago Bears. It is the second year of Netflix's Christmas Day football experiment, and the matchups are strong: the Bills are one of the league's best teams, and Packers-Bears is one of the oldest rivalries in the sport. For many households, the games will simply be on in the background while dinner cooks, which is exactly the point.

## How to plan your December

Treat a month this packed like a calendar, not a pile. Start December 1 with the Shrek rewatch, give La Bola Negra and Pride and Prejudice the first week, save 14th for a night when you want something to think about, and keep December 18, 23, and 24 clear for Ray Gunn, Cliff Booth, and Emily in Paris. One bonus worth noting: December 11 brings Bad Day, a new comedy starring Cameron Diaz as a single mother having the worst day of her life, for anyone who wants something lighter between the heavy hitters. December on Netflix has something for every night of the month, and the hardest part will be choosing what to watch first.
