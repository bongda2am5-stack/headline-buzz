Steelers Release Aaron Rodgers After His Scorched-Earth Attack on Coach Mike McCarthy

The Pittsburgh Steelers released Aaron Rodgers on Wednesday, November 25, ending one of the strangest quarterback experiments in recent NFL history less than two hours after Rodgers went on national television and accused his head coach of falling asleep during team meetings. The team announced the move in a four-word post — "We have released QB Aaron Rodgers" — that closed the book on a two-season partnership that began with Super Bowl hopes and ended with a public rift, a benching, and a 42-year-old four-time MVP suddenly out of a job with five weeks left in what he has said will be his final season.

## The 18 hours that ended it

The collapse happened fast. On Monday, November 23, head coach Mike McCarthy told reporters that "Aaron Rodgers is our quarterback." By late Tuesday night, the Steelers had announced that Rodgers would be benched and inactive for Friday's game against the Denver Broncos, with 31-year-old backup Mason Rudolph starting instead. Rodgers said he learned about the benching not from the team, but on the phone with teammates — including tight end Pat Freiermuth.

Rodgers took an excused absence on Wednesday. Then, while the Steelers were practicing, he appeared on ESPN's "The Pat McAfee Show" and unloaded on McCarthy in a way few veteran players ever have on live television. "At this point, I'm not going back to Pittsburgh," he said. "That ship has definitely sailed." About an hour later, it was official. Rodgers, who turns 43 on December 2, went unclaimed on waivers the next day and is now a free agent.

## What Rodgers said on McAfee

Rodgers described the previous week in detail, saying it summed up what he had been dealing with all season. Last Wednesday, he said, McCarthy arrived late to a quarterback meeting and within ten minutes began snoring — woke himself up, rattled his papers, and five minutes later was snoring again before walking out of the room. Rodgers said the coach then missed an offensive meeting on Thursday, a walkthrough on Friday, and a morning meeting on Saturday before the Steelers' game in Philadelphia.

"If you're sleeping in meetings, you're unhealthy," Rodgers said, calling McCarthy "unhealthy" and "unwell" and saying the behavior had been building since training camp. He said he had raised his concerns with McCarthy privately for months, "as a concerned friend," and told the coach on Monday that he should give up play-calling duties — or the team should release him.

The final straw, Rodgers said, came at halftime of Sunday's 19-16 loss to the Eagles. McCarthy, breaking down the locker-room huddle, shouted "1, 2, 3, Packers" — the name of his old team, the Green Bay Packers, where he and Rodgers won Super Bowl XLV together. McCarthy did not deny misspeaking, blaming the emotions of the game and the long hours. Rodgers also alleged McCarthy was often behind on film, even days before a game. "We have had these conversations for months now," he said.

## The numbers: what Pittsburgh is losing

Whatever the behind-the-scenes problems were, the on-field production was respectable for a quarterback in his 22nd NFL season. Rodgers started 10 games in 2026, completing 220 of 361 passes for 2,241 yards, with 16 touchdowns and six interceptions. The Steelers are 6-4 and still in the playoff race.

That is what makes the timing so striking. Teams in playoff position do not usually cut their starting quarterback the week of Thanksgiving. But the Steelers had evidently decided the relationship was beyond repair before Rodgers went on television — the benching was announced the night before, and McCarthy said later Wednesday that Rodgers had given him "an ultimatum made clearly outside of his job description."

## McCarthy and the owner answer back

McCarthy, a Pittsburgh native hired in January to replace Mike Tomlin, said after the release that there had been "continuous efforts" to reach Rodgers, whom he has known for two decades dating back to their time together in Green Bay.

Team owner and president Art Rooney II backed his coach the next day, in an interview with the Pittsburgh Post-Gazette. "The big thing, what happened this week, is insubordination can't be tolerated in any organization," Rooney said. "When you start getting ultimatums, you can't live with that." He disputed Rodgers' claims about McCarthy's health outright: "Mike's as healthy as the rest of us. Health was not an issue." He added that Rodgers had never raised any health concerns with him, and criticized airing the grievances publicly just days before a game.

## What happens now

For the Steelers, the immediate future is Mason Rudolph. The 31-year-old veteran, who has spent most of his career as a backup in Pittsburgh, starts Friday against the Denver Broncos in the Black Friday game. It is the biggest start of his career, and it comes with almost no preparation time — the team spent the week in turmoil instead of game-planning.

For Rodgers, the waiver wire has already spoken once. Because the release came after the November 10 trade deadline, he had to pass through waivers, and no team claimed him by the 4 p.m. ET deadline on Thursday. He is now an unrestricted free agent — but with most contenders settled at quarterback and only five weeks left in the regular season, the market is thin. Rodgers had said since the spring that the 2026 season would be his last in the NFL. Whether this is how his career ends, or whether another team calls, is the open question hanging over one of the greatest quarterbacks of his generation.

## What it means

The Rodgers experiment in Pittsburgh lasted less than two full seasons and produced a winning record, solid numbers, and a spectacularly messy ending. The Steelers bet that a 42-year-old quarterback, in what he called his final season, could lift a proud franchise back into contention. Instead, the partnership collapsed over trust: a quarterback who said his coach was not prepared, and a team that said its quarterback had crossed a line no organization can accept. Rodgers arrived talking about one last shot at glory — and with his career's final chapter now unwritten, nobody, including Rodgers himself, seems to know whether there is another page.
