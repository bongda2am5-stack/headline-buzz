Thanksgiving in America has two great traditions: the turkey, and the football. This year the NFL is serving up one of the strongest Thanksgiving slates in recent memory — three games, back to back, from early afternoon until late at night. The opener features two teams with identical 8-2 records. The middle game is one of the league's oldest rivalries. And the nightcap might decide who gets the top seed in the AFC. Here is everything you need to know about the day, and why each game matters.

## Game 1: Chicago Bears (8-2) at Detroit Lions (8-2), 1:00 PM ET, CBS

The day starts with a statement game. Chicago and Detroit both arrive at 8-2, tied at the top of the NFC North, and the winner takes sole possession of first place. That alone would make this worth watching. The setting makes it bigger: Detroit has hosted a Thanksgiving game almost every year since 1934, when the Lions first played on the holiday to draw a crowd. It worked so well that the tradition never stopped. Ninety-two years later, the Lions are no longer a novelty act. They are a genuine contender, and this is their biggest Thanksgiving stage in years.

Chicago's rise has been the surprise of the season. Quarterback Caleb Williams has grown into the job, the defense has been opportunistic, and the Bears have won the close games that used to slip away. Detroit counters with Jared Goff running one of the league's most balanced attacks and a home crowd that treats this game like a civic holiday. The matchup to watch is Chicago's pass rush against Detroit's offensive line: if the Bears can make Goff uncomfortable early, the Lions' rhythm offense stalls. If they cannot, Detroit will control the clock and the game. Either way, the NFC North race gets a lot clearer by 4:30 in the afternoon.

## Game 2: Philadelphia Eagles (3-7) at Dallas Cowboys (5-6), 4:30 PM ET, FOX

The middle game is about pride and survival. Philadelphia and Dallas have played on Thanksgiving before, and the rivalry needs no introduction, but this season has been unkind to both. The Eagles sit at 3-7 after a year in which almost nothing has gone to plan. The Cowboys are 5-6, still alive in the playoff race but running out of room for error. A loss here would all but end either team's realistic hopes.

Dallas has hosted a Thanksgiving game every year since 1966, and the late-afternoon slot in Arlington is one of the most watched windows in American television. The Cowboys will lean on Dak Prescott and a home crowd that expects a win in this fixture. Philadelphia, meanwhile, needs Jalen Hurts to find the form that made the Eagles a contender in recent seasons. The subplot is the trenches: Dallas has to protect Prescott against an Eagles pass rush that, even in a down year, can change a game in a single series. Expect a physical, low-scoring game — and expect the loser to spend the weekend answering questions about what went wrong.

## Game 3: Kansas City Chiefs (8-2) at Buffalo Bills (9-1), 8:20 PM ET, NBC

Save the best for last. Kansas City at Buffalo is the game of the week, and possibly the game of the season so far. The Bills lead the AFC at 9-1. The Chiefs are right behind them at 8-2. The winner takes control of the race for the conference's top seed — and with it, home-field advantage through the playoffs.

The headliner is the quarterback duel: Patrick Mahomes against Josh Allen. They have met in the playoffs, in classics, in games that came down to the final minute. Allen has the better record in the regular season; Mahomes has won when it mattered most in January. Both can win a game by themselves, and both will have to. Buffalo in late November is a different test from any other stadium in the league: cold, wind, a crowd that has waited all week for this. Kansas City's defense has been the quiet story of the Chiefs' season, and it will need its best night against Allen and a Bills offense that can score from anywhere on the field. If you only watch one game today, watch this one.

## The halftime shows

Thanksgiving football is also a television spectacle, and this year each stadium has its own halftime act. Detroit gets Wu-Tang Clan, a fitting choice for a city with deep musical roots. Dallas gets Miranda Lambert, country music in the heart of Texas. Buffalo gets the Goo Goo Dolls, a band from the city itself. The shows are short, but they are part of what makes the day feel like an event rather than just three football games.

## How to watch — and the week around today

The three Thanksgiving games air on CBS, FOX and NBC, in that order. But the holiday football week is bigger than one day. It started on Wednesday night, when the Green Bay Packers visited the Los Angeles Rams in a game streamed on Netflix. It continues on Friday afternoon, when the Denver Broncos play the Pittsburgh Steelers in the Black Friday game on Prime Video. That is five games in three days, spread across five different networks and platforms — a reminder of how fragmented watching football has become. Fans have noticed, and not always happily. Still, the audience for Thanksgiving football keeps growing, and the league keeps adding to the slate.

## Why today matters

Thanksgiving is the unofficial start of the stretch run. By Sunday night, the playoff picture will look very different from how it looked on Wednesday. Detroit or Chicago will own the NFC North. Dallas or Philadelphia will be either alive or finished. And the winner of Chiefs-Bills will have the inside track to the AFC's top seed. The games are also a reminder of why the holiday and the sport fit together: football on Thanksgiving is not an invention of television. It is a tradition older than the Super Bowl, older than most of the teams playing today, and it still delivers. Three games, three stories, one long day. Enjoy it.
