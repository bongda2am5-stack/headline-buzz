Eagles Stun Cowboys 39-38: Jake Elliott's 59-Yard Field Goal Caps the Biggest Thanksgiving Comeback Ever

Jake Elliott had already watched the other kicker make one from 59 yards. With two seconds left, he lined up to do the same — and the Philadelphia Eagles had completed the biggest comeback in Thanksgiving history, beating the Dallas Cowboys 39-38. The Eagles trailed 31-10 late in the third quarter. ESPN Analytics gave Dallas a 99.4 percent chance of winning at that point. Philadelphia scored 29 of the game's final 36 points anyway.

## The final 55 seconds

Dallas had the ball, the lead, and a chance to end it. Leading 38-36 with 55 seconds left, Cowboys head coach Brian Schottenheimer faced fourth-and-1 at the Philadelphia 39-yard line. Schottenheimer went for it instead of punting. Dak Prescott dropped back, and Eagles defensive tackle Ty Robinson broke through for the sack — a nine-yard loss and a turnover on downs.

Philadelphia took over near midfield with 50 seconds left. A pass interference call moved the ball to the Dallas 41, and with seven seconds left, Elliott trotted out for a 59-yard attempt. The kick was good — the Eagles' first lead of the game, with two seconds remaining. Dallas still had one play. Prescott's final pass was intercepted by safety Andrew Mukuba near the left sideline, a toe-tap pick that ended it.

## How a 21-point lead disappeared

Trailing 31-10 after Brandon Aubrey's third-quarter field goal, the Eagles answered with 24 seconds left in the quarter, when Joe Milton rolled right and found DeVonta Smith wide open for a 45-yard touchdown. Then the special teams struck: Robinson blocked Bryan Anger's punt and Kenny Moore II returned it for a touchdown. In minutes, a 21-point game was a seven-point game, 31-24.

Dallas answered — Prescott scrambled in to make it 38-24 — but the lead did not last. Milton ran 15 yards for a touchdown with 9:09 left to make it 38-30, though the two-point try failed. Milton then threw an interception, but Dallas could not cash in: Aubrey missed a 40-yard field goal wide left with 3:27 remaining.

Milton drove the Eagles 70 yards in just over a minute, finishing with a 24-yard touchdown pass to rookie Makai Lemon with 2:10 left. The two-point conversion failed, so Philadelphia still trailed 38-36 — setting up Schottenheimer's fateful call.

## Joe Milton, against his former team

The quarterback at the center of all of it was playing his former team in his third career start. Milton spent last season as Prescott's backup in Dallas and began this season on the Cowboys' practice squad before Philadelphia signed him on October 16, after Jalen Hurts' season ended because of a blood clot diagnosis. On Thursday he completed 21 of 31 passes for 331 yards and three touchdowns, ran for another score, and threw one interception.

All three of his touchdown passes — 50 and 45 yards to Smith, 24 yards to Lemon — came from outside the pocket, on the move. The 50-yarder traveled more than 60 yards in the air as Milton threw across his body while rolling left. His 331 passing yards were nearly half of his entire career output entering the day. "He played his butt off," head coach Nick Sirianni said. "He made some throws that only he can make."

## DeVonta Smith's career night

Milton's favorite target was DeVonta Smith, who delivered the best game of his career: 11 catches for 200 receiving yards and two touchdowns, the sixth 200-yard receiving game in Eagles history. Smith was the constant — the 50-yarder before halftime, the 45-yarder that started the rally, and the short catches on the final drive. He played through pain after landing awkwardly on a catch attempt, and still came back for the final drive.

## Dallas gave this one away

The Cowboys will spend the next 11 days — they do not play again until December 7 at Seattle — replaying the ways they lost a game they led by 21 points. The offense built the lead: Javonte Williams scored three touchdowns, and Prescott finished 19 of 28 for 291 yards and two touchdown passes. But Philadelphia's defense held Dallas to 10 points after halftime.

The fourth-and-1 call will be the one everyone remembers. Schottenheimer did not second-guess it. "I wanted the ball in Dak's hands, and unfortunately we weren't able to make it work." The missed 40-yarder will sting too, as will the blocked punt. Dallas fell to 5-7, and the loss was the largest blown lead on Thanksgiving in NFL history.

## Jones backs Schottenheimer — with a warning

Cowboys owner Jerry Jones did not say what many expected after the game. Jones said he still believes Schottenheimer is the coach who can take Dallas to a Super Bowl, and he does not anticipate coaching changes of any kind before the Seattle game. "These are setbacks, and they're damning setbacks, there's no question about it," Jones said. "Especially when we feel like that we can look at parts of the game where it was ours to take and we didn't. And so when you look at every bit of it, then you have to question everything."

Asked what gives him confidence in Schottenheimer, Jones answered: "Nothing we've seen out here tonight." He called the loss "numbing" and spread the blame: "Ownership, coaching, everybody is responsible for what we saw at the end of that game." Schottenheimer, 12-16-1 in his second season, dismissed questions about his job. "I don't worry about that. I worry about the guys in that locker room."

## What it means

For Philadelphia, the win changes the feeling of the season more than the math. The Eagles improved to 4-7, winning their second straight after seven consecutive losses. They did it with a backup quarterback and a 59-yard kick from a kicker who had been the NFL's worst over the previous two seasons. Elliott is now 4-for-5 in his career on 59-yard attempts.

At 5-7, the Cowboys have not won back-to-back games since last Thanksgiving, and they have now lost three games to backup quarterbacks this season. The 21-point collapse is the kind of loss that ends seasons — and sometimes ends coaching tenures. Jones says not this one, not yet. But he also said everything is in question. This one delivered the greatest comeback the holiday has ever seen.
