One of the most beloved love stories in English literature is coming back to the screen. Netflix's new Pride and Prejudice premieres on Thursday, December 3, 2026, as a six-part limited series starring Emma Corrin as Elizabeth Bennet and Jack Lowden as Mr. Darcy. It is the latest — and one of the most star-studded — retellings of Jane Austen's 1813 novel, arriving just in time for the holiday season, when a cozy, candlelit period romance is exactly what December evenings are for. Here is everything to know before it drops.

## The story, in brief

The Bennet family has five unmarried daughters and an estate that, under the law of the time, cannot be inherited by any of them. Mrs. Bennet's great mission is to marry her daughters well before the estate passes to a distant male cousin. Into this pressure cooker arrive two wealthy, eligible bachelors: the charming Mr. Bingley and his proud, reserved friend Mr. Darcy. Elizabeth Bennet, the sharpest of the sisters, takes an instant dislike to Darcy — and he, to his own surprise, falls for her anyway. Misunderstandings, a scheming officer named Mr. Wickham, a scandal involving the youngest sister Lydia, and a great deal of dancing follow before pride and prejudice give way to something real.

The story has survived more than two centuries because its engine is not the ballgowns or the country houses. It is the way two clever people misjudge each other, and then have to admit they were wrong — a plot that works in any era, which is why every generation gets its own version.

## The cast: a very British dream team

Casting is where Austen adaptations live or die, and Netflix has assembled one of the strongest ensembles of any version so far. Emma Corrin plays Elizabeth Bennet. Corrin broke out playing Princess Diana in The Crown and has since built a reputation for sharp, modern, slightly unpredictable performances — a good fit for a heroine whose defining quality is that she thinks for herself. Jack Lowden, known for Slow Horses, plays Mr. Darcy, a role that demands stillness and gravity: Darcy says little, feels a great deal, and has to make an audience understand both.

Around them, the supporting cast is stacked with familiar faces: Olivia Colman as Mrs. Bennet, Rufus Sewell as Mr. Bennet, Freya Mavor as Jane, Daryl McCormack as Mr. Bingley, Siena Kelly as his sister Caroline, Rhea Norwood as Lydia, newcomers Hollie Avery and Hopey Parish as Kitty and Mary, Louis Partridge as Mr. Wickham, Jamie Demetriou as Mr. Collins, and Fiona Shaw as Lady Catherine de Bourg.

## The creative team behind it

The series was written by Dolly Alderton, the bestselling author of Everything I Know About Love, who brings a distinctly modern voice to Austen's world. That pairing is the project's biggest talking point: Alderton writes about love, friendship and getting things wrong with a frankness that feels very 2026, and the question is how that sensibility lands inside a Regency drawing room. Early coverage has described the result as a fresh yet faithful adaptation — one that keeps Austen's story and wit at the center while letting the characters feel like living people rather than museum pieces.

Directing is Euros Lyn, best known for Heartstopper, with a proven touch for young romance and emotional honesty. The series is produced by Lookout Point, and the score comes from Tim Phillips and Andrew Bird. First reported in development in October 2024, it was officially announced by Netflix in April 2025, filmed in the UK in the summer of 2025, and its December 3 premiere date was announced by Alderton at the Jane Austen Festival in September.

## What makes this adaptation different

The most obvious difference is time. The beloved 2005 film told the whole novel in about two hours. A six-part series has roughly three times that room, so subplots and supporting characters that usually get cut — the Bingley sisters, Mr. Collins, Lady Catherine, the full Lydia storyline — can breathe. For readers who felt the film rushed the ending, this is the version with space for the whole book.

The second difference is tone. Netflix is positioning this as faithful to the novel rather than a reinvention, which matters after a decade of Austen riffs that modernized, gender-flipped or zombie-proofed the story. The costumes, the country houses and the ballroom scenes are all there, but the performances are meant to feel contemporary in their emotional directness — a classic adaptation for viewers who want the book, not a joke about it.

## Release details: all six episodes at once

The series premieres on Thursday, December 3, 2026, exclusively on Netflix, with all six episodes streaming at launch. There is no weekly wait: if you want to watch the whole story in one weekend, you can. A trailer has already been released, offering a first look at Corrin and Lowden in costume and a glimpse of the Bennet household, the ballroom scenes and the English countryside. The timing is deliberate: December is Netflix's biggest month of the year, and Pride and Prejudice is the centerpiece of its early-December slate.

## What to watch while you wait

Six days is not a long wait, but if you want to arrive on December 3 properly warmed up, start with the 2005 film — the version most viewers know best — and compare its two-hour sprint with the breathing room the series promises. The 1995 BBC series is the other essential reference point: six episodes itself, and still the benchmark for many fans. For something in the same spirit, Bridgerton covers the ballroom-and-scandal side of Regency England, though with far more steam than Austen ever allowed. And if you have never read the novel, it is short, funny and surprisingly modern — reading the first ten chapters will make the series land harder.

## Why it matters

Pride and Prejudice has been adapted so many times that a new version has to earn its existence, and this one has done the work before a single episode airs: a writer who understands how people actually talk about love, a director who understands young romance, and a cast with no weak link. Whether it joins the 1995 series and the 2005 film in the pantheon is something only December 3 can settle. But one thing is already clear: more than 200 years after Jane Austen published her novel, Elizabeth Bennet and Mr. Darcy can still fill a premiere week. Put the kettle on and clear your weekend — this one is about to join the debate over which version is best.
