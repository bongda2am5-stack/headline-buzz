It came down to one kick, in the worst weather, with no time left. Tyler Bass lined up from 57 yards in freezing rain at Highmark Stadium, and when the ball cleared the uprights as the clock hit zero, the Buffalo Bills had a 13-10 win over the Kansas City Chiefs. The Bills improved to 10-1, the best record in the NFL, while the Chiefs fell to 8-3.

## The final 26 seconds

The finish arrived fast, and it arrived because of special teams. With the score tied at 10 and the Chiefs punting from their own 11-yard line with 15 seconds left, Buffalo's Joe Andreessen got a piece of Matt Araiza's punt. The ball came down as a line drive, and Greg Dortch fielded it and returned it 17 yards to set up the winning kick. Four seconds remained, and Bass was on the field for a 57-yard attempt.

He made it cleanly. The officials signaled good, the clock showed zeroes, and Bills players rushed the field to mob their kicker. It was the second long kick of Bass's night: a 54-yarder early in the fourth quarter had tied the game at 10, and the 57-yarder won it. A longtime Bills columnist called it the greatest kick in franchise history.

## A kicker's night, on both sides

The contrast between the two kickers told much of the story. Bass was perfect when it mattered most, from 54 and then from 57, in rain and wind that made every kick an adventure. Harrison Butker, the Chiefs' veteran kicker and a three-time Super Bowl winner, went the other way. After Bass tied the game, Kansas City drove into position for a go-ahead field goal, only for Butker to push a 46-yard attempt wide right. It was his eighth missed field goal in 23 attempts this season.

That miss was part of a wider pattern for Kansas City. The Chiefs were penalized eight times for 68 yards, and two of those flags came on a drive that reached the Bills' 17-yard line with the score tied at 10 midway through the fourth quarter. The drive stalled at Buffalo's 28, Butker missed, and the Chiefs never scored again. Head coach Andy Reid did not hide his frustration afterward. "The offense had too many penalties, particularly in the red zone. And that set us back," he said.

## The weather won the first three quarters

This was supposed to be the game of the season: Patrick Mahomes against Josh Allen, with the AFC's top seed on the line. The weather had other ideas. Freezing rain fell all night in Orchard Park, the wind gusted, and both offenses spent the evening fighting the conditions. Kansas City finished with 241 yards of total offense, Buffalo with 230.

Allen had the rougher night. He completed 10 of 21 passes for 81 yards, with two interceptions and a passer rating of 18.3 — season lows across the board. Both interceptions came in the red zone, where Alohi Gilman and Nohl Williams picked him off and kept Kansas City in a game its offense was doing little to win. It was a night for running backs, defenses, and kickers.

## James Cook carried Buffalo

If one player kept the Bills' offense functioning, it was running back James Cook. With the passing game grounded by the weather, Buffalo leaned on the run, and Cook delivered 117 rushing yards and the game's first touchdown, a five-yard run in the second quarter that capped an 11-play, 49-yard drive and put the Bills ahead 7-0.

The Bills finished with 166 rushing yards as a team and held the ball for nearly 37 minutes, a huge edge in a game where every possession felt like a battle. They converted 9 of 16 third downs; the Chiefs managed just 2 of 11.

## Kansas City beat itself

The Chiefs will look back on this game as one they gave away. Their defense did more than enough to win: it held Allen to those season-low numbers, picked him off twice in the red zone, and kept Buffalo to 230 yards.

The penalty count hurt most. Left guard Kingsley Suamataia was flagged three times by the third quarter, including two holding calls and an unnecessary roughness penalty, and the Chiefs' eight penalties for 68 yards repeatedly pushed them backward. They went three-and-out five times. And the final sequence summed up their night: a third-down catch by Rashee Rice at the Chiefs' 20-yard line with 1:51 left was initially ruled complete for 11 yards, then overturned on review when replay showed Rice lost control of the ball as he hit the ground. Kansas City had to punt, the punt was tipped, and four seconds later the game was over.

## What it means for the AFC

The result gives Buffalo real separation in the race for the AFC's top seed. At 10-1, the Bills control their path to home-field advantage through the playoffs. The Chiefs, at 8-3, are still firmly in the playoff picture, but they now have less margin for error — and they have still beaten only one team with a winning record this season.

The schedule does not get easier for either side. Kansas City travels to face the Los Angeles Rams next Thursday night, while Buffalo visits New England on Sunday, December 6. But for one night, none of that mattered as much as the final kick.
