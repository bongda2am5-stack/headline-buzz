Liverpool are flying in Europe again. A 3-0 win away at Club Brugge on Wednesday night was not as comfortable as the scoreline suggests — head coach Andoni Iraola said so himself afterwards — but it was exactly the kind of result that defines a Champions League campaign: an early goal, a first clean sheet of the season in the competition, and two moments of late quality that turned a tight night into a statement. Alexander Isak scored twice, Florian Wirtz scored the other, and Liverpool climbed to fifth in the 36-team league-phase table with 12 points from five matches. With three games left, the road to the last 16 is now firmly in their own hands.

Isak's brace took his tally for the season to 10 goals, and Wirtz's strike made it five consecutive games in which the German has scored for club and country. But for long stretches, this was a contest. Brugge pressed and had Liverpool defending in numbers. The difference was that Liverpool took their chances and Brugge did not.

## The opener: a gift, taken ruthlessly

Liverpool struck first in the 13th minute. Brugge goalkeeper Yann Sommer played a poor pass out from the back, Bradley Barcola pounced on the mistake, and Dominik Szoboszlai's cross found Isak arriving at the far post to slot in a first-time finish from close range.

It was also the kind of goal that changes a European away night. Brugge had started brightly, but chasing the game from the 13th minute against a Liverpool side happy to defend deep and break at pace is a different proposition. The home side kept coming — Felix Lemarechal was their greatest threat — but Liverpool kept them at arm's length, and Sommer redeemed himself in part by beating away Szoboszlai's finish after a slick move involving Wirtz and Isak. At half-time, the game was still alive. A one-goal lead in Belgium is never comfortable: Liverpool became the first team to shut Brugge out in more than nine months.

## The late flourish: Wirtz, then Isak again

The decisive stretch arrived in the final 11 minutes. In the 79th minute, substitute Rio Ngumoha — on for the injured Victor Munoz in the first half — spread play from right to left for the waiting Wirtz, who took a touch to steady himself and swirled a lovely strike in off the left post from the edge of the box. It was the first time Wirtz has scored in three consecutive appearances as a Liverpool player.

Three minutes later, the game was over as a contest. A long punt forward from Alisson sent Isak breaking into space down the left wing. He moved toward Sommer and confidently dinked the ball over the goalkeeper into the net. Two goals in three minutes, both full of composure, and a tight 1-0 had become a comfortable 3-0.

Wirtz summed it up afterwards: "They put pressure on us, and in the end we won 3-0, and that maybe doesn't show what the game was. But in the end, we are happy and we showed our quality in the end." Iraola agreed. "You can think it was an easy game — and I don't think it was," he said.

## What decided it

Three things decided this game. The first was Liverpool's finishing. Isak scored with both of his big chances, and Wirtz needed one sight of goal from the edge of the box. Brugge, by contrast, had the ball in dangerous areas and could not convert.

The second was the clean sheet — Liverpool's first in this season's Champions League campaign. Alisson organised, the centre-backs dealt with crosses, and the midfield tracked runners when Brugge pushed men forward late on. Keeping a side that had averaged 2.75 goals per game in the competition scoreless, on their own ground, is a genuine achievement — and a fitting response after the shock defeat to Fenerbahce last time out.

The third was the bench. Ngumoha's introduction changed the game. The young winger's surging run set up Wirtz's goal, and his energy stretched a tiring Brugge defence. Liverpool's squad depth has been questioned at times this season; on this night, it was the difference between 1-0 and 3-0.

## What it means for the table

The win takes Liverpool to 12 points from five matches — four wins, one defeat — and up to fifth in the table. The top eight at the end of the eight-match league phase go straight through to the last 16; the teams finishing ninth to 24th must survive a two-legged play-off in February. Liverpool are now closing in on a top-eight finish that would spare them that extra round and two more high-stakes games in an already crowded calendar.

It is also worth noting how different this Liverpool side looks from the team that started the campaign. Isak has now scored in six consecutive away outings for Liverpool, and Wirtz has scored six times for club and country in November alone. The two big signings are no longer settling in — they are deciding games. Iraola said both are "in a good moment". When your two most expensive attackers are in form at the same time, the table tends to take care of itself.

## What's next

Three league-phase games remain, and Liverpool's fate is in their own hands. Win the home games, take something from the remaining away trip, and a direct route to the last 16 should follow. That is the prize: fewer fixtures, more rest, and a kinder draw when the knockout stage begins.

But Iraola's warning is worth keeping in mind. This was not a 3-0 game for 79 minutes. Brugge showed enough to remind Liverpool that in this competition, control can disappear quickly. The next European night will demand the same discipline at the back and the same ruthlessness in front of goal. If Liverpool keep pairing the two, the last 16 will not be a hope. It will be an expectation.

For now, the travelling fans can enjoy it: a clean sheet, a brace for the striker, a beautiful goal for the playmaker, and fifth place in Europe. Liverpool are flying again — and this time, they look like they know exactly where they are going.
